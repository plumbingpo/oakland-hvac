From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Most of the diagnosis comes down to finding the broken link. In Oakland homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Oakland HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians verify each stage with a meter rather than a hunch: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak-testing of gas connections, and flue draft verification, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

We work on gas, propane, and electric models from every major brand, both conventional 80 percent units and condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drains, and dual pressure switches. The truck stock covers the parts that actually fail: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so most no-heat calls end with heat the same visit. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • A furnace that shuts off every few minutes before the house warms up
  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light
  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms
  • A pilot or burner flame that is lazy yellow instead of crisp blue
  • Squeals, scrapes, or grinding noises behind the blower door
  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue
  • Loud booms at startup caused by delayed ignition

Why Hire a Professional for Furnace Repair?

The most serious furnace problem is usually hidden. A cracked or corroded heat exchanger can let combustion gases into the airstream, and nothing about it is visible from the outside of the cabinet. Delayed ignition makes things worse, because each boom at light-off puts more stress on that metal. Add high voltage, a gas valve, and a flue to the same box, and the margin for error shrinks. Diagnosis is full of traps too. A high limit that keeps tripping could be a bad switch, or it could be doing exactly its job because a filter is clogged or the blower is dying, and swapping out a working switch only hides the real fault.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

Furnace Repair Prevention Tips

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Avoid packing the utility room, because burners need open space for combustion air
  • Clear boxes, lint, and above all chlorine-based cleaners out of the furnace area
  • Replace the filter every month while the furnace is working hard, since it costs less than any other part
  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
